## Pick a model

`/model` lists the models of Claude and of Codex, each under its name. `up` and `down` pick the model, `left` and
`right` its effort, and `enter` switches.

```terminal
{accent|+- Model} {accent|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+}
{accent||} {muted|effort  default   low   medium}   {accent,bold|high}   {muted|xhigh   max}                                      {accent||}
{accent|+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+}
{accent||} {muted,bold|Claude}                                                                                   {accent||}
{accent||} {code|> 1.} {code,bold|Opus 5.5}                                                                    {muted|current} {accent||}
{accent||}      {muted|Most capable, for complex work}                                                      {accent||}
{accent||}   2. Sonnet 5                                                                            {accent||}
{accent||}      {muted|Fast and capable, for everyday work}                                                 {accent||}
{accent||}                                                                                          {accent||}
{accent||} {muted,bold|Codex}                                                                                    {accent||}
{accent||}   3. GPT-6.1-Sol                                                                         {accent||}
{accent||}      {muted|Latest workhorse model for coding and everyday work.}                                {accent||}
{accent|+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+}
{accent||} Enter {muted|select ·} Up/Down {muted|model ·} Left/Right {muted|effort ·} Esc {muted|cancel}                            {accent||}
{accent|+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+}
```

The new model applies from the next request, and later sessions start with it. Without a choice, Jinion uses Opus at
the model's default effort.

To switch straight away:

```text
/model sonnet
```

## Work with Codex

Pick a Codex model in `/model`, or type its name, to go on with Codex:

```text
/model gpt-6.1-sol
```

The conversation moves there, and Jinion says
`Switched to GPT-6.1-Sol on Codex. It reads the conversation so far with your next message.` Codex gets what you and
Claude said and did with that message, so you can carry on where you were. Picking a Claude model again moves it back
the same way. A switch happens between turns, and what ran in the background stops.

Codex works with your ChatGPT login, the one the `codex` command uses. Jinion brings Codex along, so you don't need to
install it. If you aren't signed in, switch to a Codex model and type `/account`; see
[Sign in again](#sign-in-again).

Codex works the way Claude does in Jinion: the same [permission modes](/permission-modes), plan approval, questions,
`/compact`, `/context`, `/rewind`, memory and skills. It reads `AGENTS.md`, and `CLAUDE.md` when there is no
`AGENTS.md`. Some things are Claude's only for now: background tasks, naming conversations, and `/stats`.

## Set the effort

Effort is how hard the model thinks before it answers. More effort is slower and uses more of your plan, and helps
with hard problems.

```text
/effort high
```

`/effort default` leaves it to the model, and `/effort` alone opens the `/model` panel. Not every model has effort
levels; for one without, the effort row in the panel says the model has no effort setting.

## Choose for one run

Flags and environment variables override the saved choice for one run:

```sh
jinion --model sonnet --effort low
```

| Flag             | Variable         |
| ---------------- | ---------------- |
| `--model`, `-m`  | `JINION_MODEL`   |
| `--effort`, `-e` | `JINION_EFFORT`  |

## Switch accounts

If you have more than one Claude login, such as a personal and a work one, `/account` lists them and switches between
them. `/account` shows the accounts of the agent the conversation is on; Codex has one, your ChatGPT login.

```terminal
{accent|+- Account} {muted|Claude} {accent|-------------------------------------------------------------------------+}
{accent||} {code|> 1.} {code,bold|default}                                                                     {muted|current} {accent||}
{accent||}      {muted|me@example.com · Max · 5h 92% left · 7d 80% left just now}                           {accent||}
{accent||}   2. work                                                                                {accent||}
{accent||}      {muted|ACME · Team · me@acme.dev · 5h 69% left · 7d 88% left 2h ago}                        {accent||}
{accent||}   3. client                                                                              {accent||}
{accent||}      {muted|Not signed in; press enter to sign in}                                               {accent||}
{accent||}   4. Add an account                                                                      {accent||}
{accent||}      {muted|Signs another login in, in the browser}                                              {accent||}
{accent|+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+}
{accent||} Enter {muted|switch ·} l {muted|sign in again ·} d {muted|remove ·} Up/Down {muted|move ·} Esc {muted|close}                     {accent||}
{accent|+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+}
```

Each account shows who it is signed in as and, once you have used it, its plan limits when last seen. Press `enter` on
one to switch to it. A conversation in progress carries on under the other account. The switch happens between turns,
so finish or interrupt the current one first.

`/account work` switches straight away, and later sessions start with the account you picked.

The banner shows who you're signed in as: the organization for a team plan, such as `ACME · Team`, and the email for a
personal one, such as `me@example.com · Max`. To see it in the status line too, turn on the Account segment in
[`/statusline`](/status-line).

## Add an account

Pick **Add an account** in `/account`, type a name such as `work` and press `enter`. Or type `/account add work`. A
name has letters, digits, `-` or `_`, up to 32 characters.

Jinion opens the sign-in in your browser:

```terminal
{accent|+- Account} {muted|work} {accent|---------------------------------------------------------------------------+}
{accent||} Signing in to {bold|work}. Finish it in your browser.                                           {accent||}
{accent||} {muted|If the browser didn't open: }{code|https://claude.ai/oauth/authorize?code=true&client_id=…}      {accent||}
{accent||                                                                                          |}
{accent||} {muted|Paste the code the browser shows: }{muted|code}                                                   {accent||}
{accent|+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+}
{accent||} Enter {muted|send ·} Esc {muted|cancel}                                                                  {accent||}
{accent|+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+}
```

Sign in, paste the code the browser shows and press `enter`. Jinion never sees your password. When it's done, Jinion
says `Signed in to work as me@acme.dev. Pick it here to switch.` and the account is in the list.

The `default` account is the Claude login already on your machine.

## Sign in again

When a login has expired or was signed out, its account shows `Not signed in; press enter to sign in`. Press `enter` on
it to sign in again. To sign in again to an account that is still signed in, for example with another login, press `l`
on it, or type `/account add work`.

Signing in again to the account in use doesn't end the conversation: Jinion says
`Signed in to work again as me@acme.dev. The conversation carries on with the new login.` If a turn is running, the
new login takes over when it ends.

## Remove an account

Move to the account in `/account` and press `d`. The row shows `press d again to remove`; press `d` again, or any other
key to keep it. Jinion signs the account out and removes it, and says
`Removed the work account and signed it out. Its conversations stay.`

`/account remove work` does the same without asking.

Two accounts can't be removed:

- the one in use, which shows `in use; switch to another account first`;
- `default`, the agent's own login, which shows `Claude's own login stays; l signs in again`.
