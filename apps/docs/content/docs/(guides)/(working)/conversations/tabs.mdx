---
title: Tabs
description: Keep several conversations open at once, each in a tab of its own, and move between them.
---

Each tab is a conversation of its own: its messages, its draft, its tasks and its agent. A tab you aren't looking at
keeps working.

## Open a tab

Press `ctrl+n`, or type `/tab`. A new conversation opens beside the one you were in, and a row of tabs shows above the
conversation:

```terminal
 {muted|1} Fix the flaky checkout test {border|\|} {muted|2} {accent,bold|New conversation}
```

The row shows once there are two tabs, so a single conversation keeps all the room.

`/resume` opens a saved conversation in a tab too. If that conversation is already open, its tab comes to the front.

## Move between tabs

`alt+1` to `alt+9` go to that tab. A click on a tab does too.

<Callout title="On macOS">
  `alt+1` needs Option to send Meta. In iTerm2, set it in Settings, Profiles, Keys, Left Option key, `Esc+`. In
  Ghostty, add `macos-option-as-alt = true` to its config. In Terminal, check "Use Option as Meta key" in Settings,
  Profiles, Keyboard. Clicks and `/resume` work without it.
</Callout>

## What a tab tells you

| Mark       | Means                                                     |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| a spinner  | The conversation is working                               |
| `?`        | It waits on you: a question, a permission or a plan       |

When a tab you aren't looking at waits on you, Jinion also [notifies](/notifications) you.

## Close a tab

`/close` saves the conversation and closes its tab; what it ran in the [background](/background-tasks) stops. Closing the
last tab leaves a new conversation, as `/clear` does.

`/clear` stays as it is: it saves the conversation and starts a new one in the same tab.

## Worktrees

A new tab gets a [worktree](/worktrees) of its own only when worktrees are on (`ctrl+g`), so you choose per tab. Each tab
works in its own folder: its worktree, or the project.
